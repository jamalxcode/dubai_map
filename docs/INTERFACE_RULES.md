# Dubai Real Estate Atlas: interface rules

**Last updated:** 2026-11-16 (Kuwait time)

How the page looks and behaves, with the values in force and the file that applies them. Data rules
are in [BUSINESS_RULES.md](BUSINESS_RULES.md); the goals behind both are in
[DESIGN_GOALS.md](DESIGN_GOALS.md). Keep this file in step with `assets/app/*.css` and `*.js`.

## 1. Principles

- **The rent is the interface.** Rents are written as plain dark text on the map, on a card framed in their price colour (a thick border and a light tint of it); the frame repeats what the text says, strongly enough to read from across the room, and the number stays clear whatever the colour.
- **One control per job.** One page language switch, one home picker, one view bar on the map, one area search (the rent check); the finder above the table only filters the table.
- **One core action, one next step.** "Check a rent" is first on the page; each result offers one primary button, everything else is a plain link.
- **Big and bold, but no wasted scrolling.** On wide screens the title, rent check and filters fit the first screen.
- **Honest wording.** Say what a figure is ("median" on the header's price dial; "typical" in the legend and merged labels, backed by medians; "median" where a figure is exactly one, in breakdowns and the table; "no range yet"), where it comes from and how old it is. Never "real-time", never invented examples.
- **Plain words.** Short sentences, no jargon on the page ("the middle half of tenants pay…", not "IQR").

## 2. Layout and breakpoints

| Width | Layout | Where |
|---|---|---|
| 1,100 px and up | Two panes: inputs scroll on the left (`minmax(440px,34vw)`), the map fills the window height on the right and stays put | `atlas.css` |
| 861–1,099 px | One column: title, rent check, filters, map, figures, table; the compact header (44 px title, 60 px figure) | `atlas.css` |
| 860 px and below (phones, fold phones open or closed) | One column, map in the top half of the first screen: the header shows the title and the figure only (no tagline), the rent check folds to one line until tapped, the home picker and rent range share one row; the legend starts folded; area breakdowns as a bottom sheet | `atlas.css`, `companion.css`, `atlas.js` |
| 400 px and below | Smaller title (30 px) and map buttons (32 px); photos 80% of the sheet width | `atlas.css` |
| 320 px | The narrowest screen supported and tested (no sideways scrolling, the sheet fits) | `tests/ui-check.mjs` |

- No sideways scrolling at any width. Tables scroll inside their own box (keyboard-focusable).
- **Area table:** the DLD area is a small muted second line under the area name (no column of its own); names, unit and headers wrap at word breaks, numbers never wrap; 8 px side padding. It fits without scrolling sideways in the side pane at every two-pane width (from 1,100 px: 12 px figures, 6 px side padding, the box reaching 8 px into the pane's margin) and on phones from about 540 px; a browser check measures it at 1,100, 1,350 and 1,920 px.
- The order of content below 1,100 px follows the reading order, kept with `display:contents` and `order`.

## 3. Colour and type

- **Brand palette, "golden note" (text and surfaces):** Oxblood `#521825` (primary field: header, primary buttons, selected states), Ivory `#F6F1E7` (paper and type on oxblood), Gold Leaf `#C9A227` (icons and accents: header frame, highlighted words, link underlines, focus on dark, dark-mode actions, the stale-data warning), Espresso `#2B2118` (deep text), Warm Gray `#8A7F72` (secondary, lines). Tokens: `--oxblood`, `--ivory`, `--gold`, `--espresso`, `--warmgray` (older names `--ink`, `--cream` map to espresso and ivory).
- **Contrast adjustments:** warm gray (3.5:1) and gold leaf (2.2:1) are too light for small text on ivory, so secondary text is a deeper warm gray `#655B51` (5.9:1) and gold as text on ivory is `#735B12` (5.8:1). Gold leaf reads on oxblood (5.7:1) and carries espresso type (6.5:1).
- **Prices: six blues, lowest to highest:** `#EFF3FF`, `#C6DBEF`, `#9ECAE1`, `#6BAED6`, `#2171B5`, `#084594`. Equal steps between the 5th and 95th percentile of what is shown (extremes keep the end colours); the same scale in light and dark; label badges, outlines, the rent check's colour chip and the legend ramp. Text on a badge is espresso on the four lighter blues (6.5:1 or more) and ivory `#FFFCF5` on the two darkest (5.0:1 and 9.0:1). The lowest step is almost paper-white, so swatches and badges carry a faint outline.
- **Scale:** the key runs from the 5th to the 95th percentile, so a few extreme areas don't stretch it. The legend writes a value at all seven step edges (38k, 52k … 120k); below the first value takes the lightest blue, above the last the darkest.
- **Dark mode:** follows the device until the visitor picks; remembered (`theme`). The base map switches with it: OpenFreeMap **Liberty** (detailed, in colour) by day, OpenFreeMap **Dark** lifted to the site's night colours (`tuneDark` in atlas.js) at night. The CSS invert filter is only for the OpenStreetMap fallback tiles.
- **Contrast:** WCAG AA (4.5:1) in light, dark and Arabic, checked by axe on every push. In dark mode price labels stay light ivory cards, so their frames show exactly their key colour, a little smaller (16 px figures) with a thin dark edge, so they don't glare on the dark map. Figures are espresso on a light card in every band, so their contrast doesn't depend on the price colour.
- **Type:** Barlow (text) and Barlow Condensed (headings, figures), served from the site. Arabic letters come from the device's Arabic font; numbers stay in Barlow. No letter-spacing in Arabic (it breaks letter joining).
- **Figures:** tabular numbers; AED written before the amount; Western digits in both languages.

### Side panel system (`assets/app/panel.css`, loaded last)

One look for every block of the side panel (and the same blocks on phones): sections on the page's paper divided by one 1 px hairline (no cards, no black rule, no beige table head); one heading style (Barlow Condensed 22 px bold); one field style (40 px high, 42 px on phones, 8 px corners, a lighter paper `--paper`, a gold focus ring); labels 12 px semi-bold muted on one line; spacing from 4, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32 px. Filters: Home and Near side by side, the budget (its exact boxes folded) under them across the panel, the budget line under that (phones: Home and budget on the first row so the map stays high; one column below 340 px). The rent check is a section, not a card: area, rent ("Optional, e.g. 75,000" as its hint) and Check on one row. Key figures in three equal columns on a hairline. The table has no box: a sticky head on the paper, hairline rows, a hover tint. Motion: one entrance on load (GSAP: sections settle from 14 px below, 0.07 s apart), nothing else moves on its own.

## 4. Map labels

- Each area: the figure (`AED 65k`) in plain espresso type with the area name underneath, on an ivory card framed by a 3.5 px border in its price-key colour, tinted with 17% of it, and a 1 px dark hairline outside (so the palest blue still shows on a light map). Thin figures: the frame is dashed. Groups: a second frame offset behind, in the same colour (a stack of cards). Hover and focus add a gold ring outside.
- **Names as shown:** names registered in capitals ("TOWN SQUARE") show in title case ("Town Square"), keeping acronyms (JLT, JVC, DIFC…) (`T.place`, the same rule on area pages); search, links and saved areas keep the registered name. Names are shortened once in the script (single areas at 28 letters, groups at 22 plus "+N"); the CSS doesn't cut them again.
- Labels never overlap. Overlapping labels merge into a group label coloured by the price key like any other (their contract-weighted average), marked by a gold stacked-card outline; every group names its busiest area and how many others it holds ("JLT + 12 areas", "+ 1 area"; `groupName`). At the opening zoom labels sit 4 px apart (`pad`), so a few more areas show before merging.
- Label boxes are measured as drawn (a hidden label with the same styles, re-measured once the fonts load), so placement holds in both themes and languages.
- A group of up to 3 comes apart when each label fits at its point or just beside it; a moved label has a thin line to a dot at its point.
- Labels keep clear of the zoom bar, the view bar and the legend, and are re-placed when a control appears, the legend folds or the map is panned (not while a breakdown is open; then once it closes).
- **No label is ever drawn on a map control** (any `.leaflet-control`: view bar, zoom, credits, full-screen button, budget slider; and the legend), in any view, at any width. A label that can't step aside joins a nearby neighbour if the joined label has room, else it is hidden until the view gives it room (`hidden` in labels.js rule 3). The browser check switches through every view at 320, 344 and 375 px.
- A label whose point is on the map stays fully inside its edges (a line joins it to its point if it had to slide); crowded edges merge rather than cut labels off.
- **Thin figures** (fewer than 10 contracts): a dashed outline (the price colour darkened slightly) and an italic figure; same colours and contrast.
- **Community outlines** under the labels: official boundaries, filled with the area's price colour at 32% and outlined at 70%, redrawn with every view, filter and type change.
- Labels are keyboard buttons: Tab to one, Enter or Space opens it; the spoken name is the visible text.
- **Focus on an area:** tapping one area's label (or Enter, or the rent check's area) flies to its official outline, darkens the map outside it (black at 42%), rings it in gold leaf (3.5 px) and opens its breakdown; other labels stay at full contrast. **The breakdown never covers the area** (`openArea`, `fitFocus` in atlas.js): the card opens first, then the map fits the area into the room it leaves. On computers the card hangs from the area's top edge (tip pointing at it), or sits to its right (no tip) when that shows the area larger, as for tall areas on a wide map; it is at most 58% of the map's height and the map fits again if photos make it taller. On phones the area fits in the strip above the sheet (the map is brought to the top of the screen first). Closing the breakdown or zooming by hand ends it; a home or view change keeps it. Group labels zoom in until their areas separate.
- Clicking a group zooms in until it splits; at full zoom it lists its areas.
- Rules live in `assets/app/labels.js` (no DOM) and are unit-tested.

## 5. Map controls

- **Budget mode** (`draw` and `budgetNote` in atlas.js): with a Min or Max set, only the areas whose yearly rent for the chosen home falls in the range keep their labels and count in the figure at the top; their outlines light up (gold-leaf edge, price colour at 60%) and the others turn grey (warm gray edge, dark fill at 22%). Colours stay those of the whole map, so they match the key. The range is on rent in every view. A line under the filters says how many areas fit, or that none does with a hint; Min above Max is swapped and said so. The table follows the same range.
- **Compare two areas** (`startCompare`, `openCompare`, `renderCompare`, `animateCompare` in atlas.js): "Compare" next to "Check a rent here" in a breakdown, and among the rent check's links. An oxblood bar over the map asks for the second area (tap a label, or one of the three nearest with figures for the chosen home; Cancel). It opens over the map (computers: a 420 px panel on the right; phones: a sheet up to 60% of the screen, the map scrolled to the top). Layout, a "versus" sheet: a slim oxblood header with a gold-leaf rule; the two areas facing each other (A in gold leaf, B in oxblood, rose at night), each with its name and the rent rolling up (no photos: Commons photos near a point are often of people, which reads as random side by side) (the header's odometer); one sentence saying which is cheaper and by how much (about the same within 3%); then mirrored rows, A's value | label | B's value, with two 4 px bars growing out from the centre, each as long as its value against the larger; the better value (lower rent and price, higher yield) in bold; rent by home size with short names (tap one to switch Home); the distance; Open A, Open B, Copy link. Motion (GSAP): the panel slides in, the sides and rows settle, the bars grow. Swap (⇄), ×, Escape or a tap on the map closes it; the link keeps `compare=A|B`.
- **Hotels** (`loadHotels`, `drawHotels`, `openHotel`, `hotelLegend` in atlas.js): a fourth view, **Hotels**, after Rent · Sale price · Yield, shown once `hotels.json` loads (`?view=hotels` keeps it). Labels count places to stay per area (`T.count(n,'hotel')`): the same light cards, the count on an **oxblood** badge with ivory type (the header band's colour; the blue scale is for prices only), in both themes; outlines are washed and edged in **gold leaf** `#C9A227` (fill 20%). Every place is a small **gold-leaf dot ringed in oxblood** (ringed in ivory at night) (`.hdot`), so it can't be taken for a station (stations are paper dots ringed in their line's colour); its name shows on hover; a tap opens a breakdown: name, kind, stars, area, **Google Maps** and **Prices on Booking.com** links (styled like every link in a breakdown: espresso with a gold-leaf underline, pills on phones), and the OpenStreetMap credit with "prices are set by the booking site, not this page". The budget and the thin marks don't apply. The legend says how many places, gives the official city-wide average room rate and occupancy with its month once DET figures exist (otherwise one line saying they come with Dubai Pulse access), and credits OpenStreetMap. The figure at the top is "Hotels in view".
- **An area's card in the Hotels view** (`hotelSection` in atlas.js): leads with the area's places to stay, not its rent: the typical hotel price a night (with prices), how many places and how many have a price, then a table of up to 12 places (dearest first once prices exist, else by stars, then name) with kind, stars (column only when one has a rating) and price a night; each name is a button that opens that place's card (zooming to 15 first). A Booking.com search for the area follows. The rents fold under "Rents in this area" (`details.rentMore`), with "Check a rent here" and "Compare" inside the fold (`onPopup` in companion.js), since both are about rent. Other views keep the rent card with a one-line places-to-stay note.
- **The price dial** (header, `setDial` in atlas.js): an arc on the price key's blues with an ivory, gold-edged knob at the median of what's on screen, between the lowest and highest area in view (written at the two ends, e.g. 35k and 156k). In the middle: what it is ("Median rent", "Median sale price", "Median yield", "Median a night"), the figure, and its unit ("AED a year", "AED", "gross a year", "AED a night"), in uppercase small text (no letter-spacing in Arabic). The caption says what's in view and that the ends are the lowest and highest area. It follows the view: yearly rent, sale price, yield, and the Hotels view's price a night once hotel prices exist. Counts (contracts, places to stay) hide the dial and show the number. 200 px wide, 180 in the side pane, 150 at tablet widths, 156 on phones; drawn left to right in Arabic too.
- **Night prices without per-hotel prices** (the owner's choice, 18 Nov: options 2 and 4):
  - **Official rate (DET):** once `hotel_stats.json` exists (with the Dubai Pulse key), the Hotels view's dial shows "Dubai average a night": the latest month's average room rate for all of Dubai, between the lowest and highest month of the past year; the caption names the month and source. A hotel's card says "Dubai's average room rate in {month}: AED … a night (all hotels, DET)".
  - **A booking partner's widget:** once `assets/app/partners.js` has an https widget link, each hotel's card offers "Show live prices from {partner}". Nothing from the partner loads until it is tapped; then the partner's own panel opens in the card (sandboxed frame, 320 px high) for that hotel, two weeks ahead, one night. Their prices, never stored here. Until set, the status page lists it under "Needs you".
- **Without hotel prices** the figure at the top is a count and says so: "809 places", with "Places to stay in view · N areas · nightly prices on booking sites" under it, so it never reads as a price where prices usually go.
- **Exploring for fun** (`assets/app/fun.js`, `fun.css`; the owner's request, 20 Nov), all from real data, nothing that keeps score:
  - **"Dubai right now"**, a strip after the filters (above the map on phones): tappable facts (cheapest 1-bed; cheapest 1-bed a 10-minute walk from the Metro; best yield; biggest rent rise, once history allows; cheapest night in a hotel rated 4.5+ from 5+ reviews; dearest holiday homes), each switching to its view and home and opening its answer; then **Take the 60-second tour** and **Guess the rent**. Facts skip industrial areas and figures from fewer than 10 contracts.
  - **First-visit tips**: three, one at a time, in the strip (never over the map), "Next tip" / ×, remembered (`funTip`).
  - **Guess the rent**: a well-measured area (300+ one-bed contracts), a slider across the one-bed range, Reveal: the median, its contracts, and how far off the guess was (% and AED), then Another area / Show it on the map. No score or streak.
  - **The tour**: up to six stops picked from the data (busiest market, dearest, cheapest, best yield, dearest to buy, cheapest hotel nights), each flying the map there in its view with one caption; Back / Next stop / End / Open this area; Escape ends it. Never advances by itself.
  - **"You"**: after a rent check with a rent, an oxblood pill under the area's label: "You: AED 75k · 7% above" (or below, or at the median); replaced by the next check, restored when the check resumes.
  - **Count-up**: when the view or home changes, single labels' figures count up to their value (0.6 s, from 55%); off with reduced motion.
  - **Budget slider**: bottom left on the map (above the map on phones, under the view chips, so it never covers a label), in the Rent and price-priced stay views: "Up to AED 63,000 a year" / "a night", the far end "Any budget"; it sets the Max box and follows it.
  - **A card's headline fact**: a gold-edged pill under the name when it stands out: the cheapest / dearest area for the home, among the 10% cheapest / dearest, the best yield (Rent, Sale price and Yield views; solid areas, 20+ to compare).
  - **Rent or buy?** in Sale price and Yield cards: deposit (20–80%) and mortgage rate (2–8%) sliders; renting a month and a year, buying (deposit, about 6% fees, the monthly payment on a 25-year loan) and the price in years of rent; marked an estimate, not advice.
  - **Save as image** beside Share link on every card: a 1200×630 picture (title band, name, the figure framed in its key colour, what it is, data date, site address), shared as a file on phones or downloaded.
  - **Find a hotel / holiday home** in the places-to-stay side panel: the five cheapest within the nightly budget, filtered by rating (any, 4+, 4.5+), each opening its card.
  - **Dots** for hotels and holiday homes start at zoom 12 (the city-wide view is the labels').
- **An area's card follows the view** (`popup`, `ownSection`, `salesNote` in atlas.js): **Rent** leads with rent (the picked home's median a year, then all homes, the rent trend, the table by home size); sales get one small line, "Buying here: median sale price AED … · gross yield …% (the picked home's)", with a "Show sale prices" button that switches view; no sale chart. **Sale price** leads with the sale median (picked home, then all homes with the change on a year ago), the sale price chart and a table by home size (sale price, annual rent, yield); every rent figure there says "Annual rent". **Yield** leads with the gross yield and how it is made (annual rent against the sale price), with the same table. In both, the full rent breakdown folds under "Annual rents in this area". Sale prices round to the thousand.
- **Share a card** (`shareLine`, `shareURL`, `shareText`, `openFromURL` in atlas.js): every area, hotel and holiday home card ends its links with a line "Send this area (hotel, holiday home) to someone:" and a **Share link** button. On phones it opens the phone's share sheet; elsewhere it copies a line of text saying what it is ("Jumeirah Village Circle: median rent AED 70,000 a year (1-bedroom apartments), from Ejari contracts"; a hotel's price with the date checked) and the link, and says "Copied: paste it in a message" (if copying is blocked, the link shows in a box, selected). The link is the page's own address (so it follows the site to its own domain) with `?view=…&home=…&open=a:<area>` (`h:`/`s:` + name|lat|lng for a hotel or holiday home, `&lang=ar` in Arabic); opening it opens that card once its data is in (up to 12 s; a place since gone just opens the map), and the rent check and the saved map position step aside for it.
- **Beside the dial, in bold** (`dialSide` in atlas.js, `.dialSide`): what the figure is, in large gold-on-oxblood capitals (the owner's request, 20 Nov), with one muted line under it: Rent "AED A YEAR" (median yearly rent, the home), Sale price "MEDIAN SALE PRICE", Yield "GROSS A YEAR (ROI)", Hotels and Holiday homes "AED A NIGHT"; hidden when there is no dial (counts). A million-dirham figure gets smaller type so it stays inside the ring (`.long`).
- **Layer switches:** Metro and A+ع (English and Arabic place names) are on/off layers, so they carry a small switch (track and knob; oxblood when on, gold at night) and never fill like a chosen view.
- **View chips on phones** (`placeViews`, `.viewChips` in atlas.js): at 860 px and below the five views leave the map for a row of pill chips just above it (they wrap onto a second row; all show in English and Arabic); the map's top-right bar keeps Metro and place names. Wider, the views stay in the bar, which is given the room beside the zoom buttons and scrolls (`fitViewBar`). The phone area check runs the full width; the header caption is short ("1-bedroom apartments · 128 areas · ends: cheapest and dearest area"); photo captions read "Photo by {author}" (author and licence in full on hover and when opened); the dial's track is a little lighter so it shows on the oxblood band.
- **Places-to-stay views follow through the page** (`stayPanel`, `stayFacts`, `stayTable`, `swapRange` in atlas.js; `.stayMode` on the root): in Hotels and Holiday homes the home picker and the rent check are hidden; the budget label reads "Nightly budget (AED)" and filters areas by their typical price a night (the rent budget and the nightly one are kept apart: switching views swaps the boxes' values); the walk filter and the budget share the first row. The key figures read "Hotels with a price" (or holiday homes) with its areas, "Dubai median a night" and "Cheapest area" (from areas with 3+ priced places, else marked "few places"). The table is "Hotel prices by area" / "Holiday home prices by area": Area (a button that opens it), Typical, Cheapest, Dearest, Places; cheapest first; areas with fewer than 3 places in italics; Cheapest and Dearest hide on phones; the CSV follows. Labels give full figures ("AED 1,036"); areas with fewer than 3 priced places are dashed and left out of the dial's ends, whose numbers are full too; the caption reads "Hotels in view · N areas · ends: cheapest and dearest area". Priced hotels are solid gold dots; with prices, those without one are small hollow warm-gray rings drawn only from zoom 14. The legend shows the key, the counts (and the ring), Dubai's trend line once it exists, then a folded "About these prices" (method, credits, "history kept since"). Cards: lists cheapest first (priced before unpriced), a hotel's kind only when not "Hotel", ratings only from 5 reviews (hotels too, from Tripadvisor via Xotelo), a cross-link line to the other view's typical price with a button that switches view, the nights priced and dates as "19 Nov 2026"; on phones the prices lead the sheet (photos and Google links stay below). With five views the view bar is given the width beside the zoom buttons and scrolls (`fitViewBar`); long names wrap in lists (fixed table layout).
- **Price history** (`loadStayHist`, `stayTrend` in atlas.js), from `stay_history.json`, loaded the first time a places-to-stay view is drawn: an area's hotel or holiday home breakdown shows, under its typical price, a small gold line chart "Typical hotel price a night, {first day} to {last day}" (the same chart as the rent trend) and, once a day at least 4 weeks earlier exists with 3+ places on both days, "+9% since {day}" (up and down coloured as elsewhere). With fewer than 3 days it says when the history started. The legend of each view carries the same line for all of Dubai.
- **Holiday homes** (`loadHomes`, `homesBy`, `drawHomes`, `openHome`, `homeSection`, `homeNotes` in atlas.js): a fifth view after Hotels, shown once `holiday_homes.json` has a current price (`?view=homes` keeps it). A price view like any other: labels "AED 420 a night" on the blue scale, the dial "Median a night" with the lowest and highest area, the key "Typical holiday home price a night" with its notes. Each place is a small **espresso dot ringed in gold leaf** (ringed in ivory at night) (`.hhdot`), so it can't be taken for a hotel (gold ringed in oxblood) or a station. A tap opens its card: name, "Serviced apartment or holiday home", rating and reviews, area, average and range, then Google Maps, **Prices on Tripadvisor** (only an https tripadvisor link) and Booking.com, with the source line. An area's breakdown in this view leads with its typical holiday home price, the range, and the list (each name opens its card); rents fold under "Rents in this area".
- **Hotel prices** (`hotelPrice`, `hotelNotes`, `areaHotels` in atlas.js), once `hotel_prices.json` exists: the Hotels view becomes a price view. Labels read "AED 367 a night" on the blue price scale (badges, outlines and key, like every price), from the area's median hotel price; the key is titled "Typical hotel price a night" with the hotel notes under it; the figure at the top is "Typical hotel price a night in view". A hotel's tooltip adds its price; its card leads with "Average AED 1,621 a night" and says what was priced (lowest price on booking sites for one room for 2, number of sample nights, their range, the date checked); a hotel without a price says it isn't listed on booking sites yet. An area's hotel card adds "From AED … to … a night" (its cheapest and dearest priced hotel) under the typical price. Prices checked more than 10 days ago are not shown. Every area's breakdown, in any view, has a line "Places to stay: 33 · typical AED 367 a night (24 with a price)" with a gold-leaf rule. Credits: OpenStreetMap, and booking sites via Xotelo (TripAdvisor).
- **Metro and Tram** (`drawTransit`, `openStation` in atlas.js): a **Metro** button in the map's top-right bar beside the place-names button (a layer switch, so not in the zoom stack, which keeps zoom, All Dubai and Near me) (filled when on, remembered as `transit`), shows the Metro (4 px) and the Tram (3 px), each on an ivory casing, slimmer as the map zooms in (`transitScale`: full size to zoom 11, 10% less per zoom level, half at zoom 16). Station dots by `stationScale`: 40% of full size at zoom 8, 55% at 10, 65% at 11, 80% at 12, full at 13–14, then 90% and 80% closer (full size: 4.5 px radius, tram 3.6, interchanges 6) (espresso `#1E1712` at night); stations are paper dots ringed in their system's colour, interchanges larger, with a name tooltip (station and line). Tapping a station opens its breakdown (lines as coloured chips, the typical figure for the chosen home within the walk, the areas with distance; tapping one focuses it). The **Near Metro / Tram** filter (Anywhere, 10-minute walk, 20-minute walk) sits after the budget, turns the lines on, keeps only areas near a station (as the budget does) and adds the cheapest station to the line under the filters. Hidden until `transit.json` loads. Two colours from the map's theme, one per system: **Metro oxblood `#6E2230`**, **Tram gold leaf `#B8901F`**; at night `#D99AA5` and `#D9B44A`. Both Metro lines share the Metro colour; the line is named in the tooltip and breakdown.
- **Map edge tab** (`setWide` in atlas.js), halfway down the map's edge next to the side panel (left in English, right in Arabic): on computers (1,100 px and up) an arrow that slides the side panel away so the map fills the window, and back; remembered on the device (`mapWide`). On narrower screens a full-screen toggle (corner icon) that covers the page until tapped again or Escape; never remembered. The map keeps its centre; labels keep clear of the tab. ## 6. The rent check

- Fields: area (search with nicknames, DLD names and typos), optional yearly rent (written with thousands separators as you type), **Check**. No home field: the check uses the map's **Home** picker.

- Result: what was checked, the big figure with a small chip in its price colour, what it is and how many contracts, the middle half when known, the verdict if a rent was entered, up to four findings ("Dearer than 64% of 126 areas…"; on phones folded under **More about this area**, open on wider screens), a folded **What does this mean?** (what a median is, why there is no range yet), one primary button and plain links (Save · Show on the map · Share · Dubai Data page). - **History and cross-check notes:** under the figures, "Rent +6.5% since 14 Nov 2025" (bold, no colour: a rise is bad news for tenants and good news for owners); a jump not yet confirmed says so in the warning colour, and its label is dashed like a thin figure (the legend says "or a big change waiting to be confirmed"); "Other sources differ by more than 15%" lists the source, home and their figure, with a line on why sources differ.
- **The picked home leads:** with a home size picked, the breakdown starts with that home's figure (the one on the label), with its yield and sale price, highlights its row in the unit table, and gives the all-homes figures in a smaller block under it.
- **Wide screens:** a Leaflet pop-up, up to 320 px wide, its tip just above the label so the label stays visible; a 32 px × close button. It is never taller than the clear space between the map's buttons and the legend (`maxHeight`, set with the pan margins); longer content scrolls inside the card. When its photos arrive it is laid out and panned again (`refitPopup`), since it grew after opening. Browser-checked at 960×620 to 1,440×900. It never opens under the map's buttons or the legend: Leaflet draws controls above pop-ups, so the pop-up's pan margins are measured from the controls (left column, top-right stack, legend) and the map pans to clear them (`popupMargins` in `atlas.js`, rechecked on resize, language button and redraw).
- **Trend lines** (rent by month, sale price per sq ft) carry a small scale: lowest and highest value at the left (thousands as "k" above 10,000), first, middle and last month underneath, in 10 px muted text; drawn left to right in Arabic too.

## 8. Language and direction

- English and Arabic. Chosen by a `?lang=` link, else the saved choice, else the device; set before first paint to avoid a flash in the wrong direction.
- **Language switch** (header, next to the theme button): **English | عربي** both always shown, the current one filled white; tapping the other reloads the page in that language and remembers it (`lang`).
- Every visible text goes through `T()`; fixed markup text has a `data-t` key. A missing translation shows English, and the tests fail on it.
- CSS uses logical properties (`margin-inline-end`, `inset-inline-start`, `text-align:start`) so the page mirrors without separate rules.
- Area names stay as registered in Ejari (English); home types are shown in Arabic but stored in English.
- No letter-by-letter animation in Arabic.

## 9. Motion

- One opening moment: the title sets in letter by letter (English only), the headline number rolls up.
- Labels fade in after a filter change; zooming redraws instantly.
- Sheets slide up; the map flies to a checked area.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` turns all of it off (jumps instead of flights, no animation).

## 10. Feedback and states

- Loading: "Loading rents…"; a shimmer where photos will appear.
- Errors offer a way forward: "Try again", suggestions for an unknown area, the units that have figures.
- Stale data: a gold-leaf warning sign when the data is more than 7 days behind; hideable for the visit.
- Empty: a message that says what to change ("Clear the search or widen the rent range").
